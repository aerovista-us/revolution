
2️⃣ “Gottman Companion Sheet” for Therapists

You can format this as a 1–2 page PDF / handout.

Title:

Gottman Companion to “After the Alarm” — A Three-Track Nervous System Arc

Purpose:
This sheet helps therapists, coaches, and facilitators use the “After the Alarm” sequence as a springboard for psychoeducation and guided conversation, without turning the session into a lecture.

A. Track–to–Concept Map

Track 1 – “My Chaos Ain’t Your Crisis”

Core dynamics:

Negative Sentiment Override

Defensiveness as emotional control

Flooding and harsh start-ups

Clinical doorway:

Ask: “Where do you recognize turning a neutral comment into an attack?”

Explore the cost of making one partner responsible for both people’s emotions.

Track 2 – “I Don’t Hear Danger In Your Voice”

Core dynamics:

Physiological soothing and de-escalation

Co-regulation and consistent presence

Differentiating past threat from current partner

Clinical doorway:

Ask: “What signals of safety does your body notice here?”

Identify specific behaviors that help each partner’s nervous system stand down.

Track 3 – “We Don’t Flinch We Build”

Core dynamics:

Turning insight into new behavior

Shared meaning and future orientation

Moving from “Don’t hurt me” to “Let’s design this together”

Clinical doorway:

Ask: “If you weren’t busy surviving this relationship, what would you build in it?”

Invite couples to name rituals, values, and long-term visions.

Optional Final Track – “After We Learned to Hear Each Other”

Core dynamics:

Secure-functioning baseline

Trust as competence, not blind faith

Reduced hypervigilance in communication

Clinical doorway:

Ask: “What would everyday life feel like if this track described you?”

Use it as a north-star for regulation work.

B. Suggested Session Flow Using the Tracks

Pre-Listening Setup

Normalize: “We’re going to listen to stories that might feel familiar, and then notice what they stir up.”

Invite regulation strategies (breath, pause button, etc.).

Track 1 Debrief – Defensiveness & Control

Questions:

“Where do you see yourself in this narrator?”

“How does your partner experience your ‘storm’ side?”

Gottman tie-in:

Briefly describe Negative Sentiment Override and how it colors perception.

Homework idea:

Each partner writes 3 examples of neutral moments they mis-read as hostile.

Track 2 Debrief – Safety & Co-Regulation

Questions:

“What does the ‘first moment of safety’ feel like in your body?”

“What does your partner do that helps you not brace?”

Gottman tie-in:

Discuss physiological flooding vs. calm; heart-rate, time-outs, repair attempts.

Homework idea:

Build a shared list of 5 small “safety signals” they can intentionally use.

Track 3 Debrief – Design & Shared Meaning

Questions:

“If your relationship were a project you’re co-designing, what’s the blueprint?”

“What are you building that’s bigger than any one fight?”

Gottman tie-in:

Introduce shared meaning, rituals of connection, and life dreams work.

Homework idea:

Create a 1-page “We Don’t Flinch, We Build” statement with 3 values + 3 practices.

Optional Final Track – Integration

Questions:

“What would have to change in your day-to-day interactions for this song to feel like your normal?”

Action:

Turn their answers into 1–2 concrete behaviors per person.

C. Therapist Notes / Cautions

Avoid shaming defensiveness.
Frame it as outdated protection, not a character flaw.

Anchor in bodily experience.
Keep circling back to: “Where do you feel this in your body?”

Use the music as a third object.
When emotions spike, you can always redirect to:
“In the song, what did you notice…?” instead of “You always…”

D. Optional Handout Snippet for Clients

These songs aren’t here to tell you what’s wrong with you.
They’re here to help your nervous system recognize patterns it already knows.
If a line hits hard, it’s not an accusation — it’s an invitation to look at how you learned to protect yourself, and whether those protections still serve the life you want now.
